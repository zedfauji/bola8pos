<tasks>

<task type="checkpoint:human-verify" gate="blocking">
  <name>Checkpoint 1: On-screen money rendering across POS, caja, and reports in both locales</name>
  <what-built>
    Every money and percent string in the app now renders through `src/shared/lib/format.ts`, which reads the signed-in staff member's locale from the Phase 21 i18n singleton and prefixes a locale-specific currency symbol — a two-character prefix for es-MX, a single character for en-US — in front of an `Intl.NumberFormat`-grouped amount. The old locale-blind formatter in `domain-helpers.ts` is deleted. A committed ESLint rule now blocks any new ad-hoc money formatting.

    Two intended, user-visible changes to look for rather than report as bugs: the es-MX currency prefix is longer than before, and amounts of one thousand or more now carry a locale digit-grouping separator that they did not carry before.
  </what-built>
  <how-to-verify>
    Start the app with `npm run tauri dev` and sign in with the admin PIN `0000`.

    First pass, default es-MX locale:
    1. `/pos` — open a tab, add an item with a price-changing modifier. Confirm the modifier delta shows exactly one currency symbol with an explicit plus, the cart line totals look right, and nothing overflows or wraps awkwardly in the cart panel.
    2. Open the payment modal and switch it to split mode. Confirm the per-row charges label, the remaining-balance figure, and the over-by warning each show one symbol and the correct sign.
    3. `/pool-tables` — start a timer and open a table. Confirm the live-charge overlay shows exactly one currency symbol. Before this phase it showed two; that is the fix, not a regression.
    4. `/reports` — walk the tabs that show money: caja report, refunds register, combo mix, modifier popularity, payment methods, hourly breakdown. Confirm no doubled symbol, no black-on-black or clipped table cell, and that expense rows read as negative while income rows read as positive.
    5. Caja dashboard — confirm the opening-cash figure, the entry rows with their explicit signs, and the expense/income totals.
    6. `/settings` → Tip Split — confirm the example breakdown preview shows one symbol per figure.

    Second pass, en-US locale:
    7. `/settings` → Language, switch to en-US, save. The app re-renders without a reload.
    8. Revisit `/pos` and `/reports`. Confirm the currency prefix is now the single-character form and that every figure that had a symbol still has exactly one.
    9. Switch back to es-MX before finishing.

    Report anything that shows two symbols, no symbol, a wrong sign, clipped or overflowing text, or a figure that changed by a factor of a hundred.
  </how-to-verify>
  <resume-signal>Type "approved" if every screen is correct, or list the screens and what was wrong on each.</resume-signal>
</task>

<task type="checkpoint:human-verify" gate="blocking">
  <name>Checkpoint 2: Printed and exported artifacts — receipt, pre-cheque, cash count, PDF</name>
  <what-built>
    The printed and exported artifacts — thermal receipt, pre-cheque, printed cash count, and PDF export — now format money against the locale their build function was called with rather than the live UI locale, using the same shared formatter. The pool-rate line on a pre-cheque and the PDF's local money helper both moved onto it as part of this.

    Receipt column math in `receipt-format.ts` measures UTF-8 bytes, and the es-MX prefix adds two ASCII bytes to every money line on a 32-column receipt. Column alignment on real paper is the specific risk this checkpoint exists to catch.
  </what-built>
  <how-to-verify>
    1. Open a tab with several items, at least one over one thousand so digit grouping is exercised, and print a pre-cheque. On the physical slip (or the print preview if no printer is attached), confirm the money column stays right-aligned, no line wraps unexpectedly, and the pool-rate line reads sensibly with its per-hour suffix.
    2. Close the tab with a payment and print the receipt. Confirm the same alignment properties and that the receipt's text labels and its money figures are in the same language — that consistency is the specific defect this phase's locale-parameterized formatter was added to prevent.
    3. From the caja dashboard, print the cash count. Confirm its fixed-width column padding still lines the amounts up.
    4. From `/reports`, export any PDF that contains money. Open it and confirm the currency prefix renders as readable characters rather than boxes or missing glyphs, and that no money column is clipped.
    5. Optional but valuable: switch a staff member's locale to en-US, reprint a receipt for a tab created earlier, and confirm the receipt is internally consistent in one language rather than mixing labels from one locale with a symbol from the other.

    Report any wrapped line, misaligned column, missing glyph, or receipt that mixes two locales.
  </how-to-verify>
  <resume-signal>Type "approved" if every printed and exported artifact is correct, or describe what was wrong and on which artifact.</resume-signal>
</task>
